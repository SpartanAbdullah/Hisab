## 1. Competitor landscape — three clusters, and which one Hisaab actually competes in

### Cluster A — Western split apps (Splitwise, Settle Up, Tricount, Splid)

This cluster is mid-upheaval, which matters because upheaval creates switchers:

- **Splitwise** — the category incumbent with the deepest feature set (100+ currencies, itemized OCR, simplify-debts, cross-group net balances) has throttled its free tier to ~3 expenses/day with unskippable interstitial ads; the daily cap is "the single biggest driver of user churn and of the current wave of 'Splitwise alternative' apps" (profile: pricing/complaints). Its reminders are email-only (no push nudges) and any member can delete a shared expense for everyone (profile: recordManagement, weaknesses).
- **Settle Up** — best settle-up mechanics in the market (fewest-transfers algorithm, "who should pay next", weighted shares), true offline with auto-sync, ~1M MAU at 2025 peak — but its 2025 redesign brought crash/join-bug/UI-regression backlash and ads angered the loyal base (profile: strengths, complaintThemes).
- **Tricount** — bunq-owned and now completely free ("No limits. No ads. No fees."), shipping a Splitwise-migration importer to harvest paywall refugees — but it *deleted* CSV/PDF export, sorting, and personal-expense mode in v8, suffered months of 2025 sync bugs (expenses vanishing/reappearing), and auto-creates bunq bank accounts at signup: the product is a neobank funnel (profile: pricing, featureDepth, complaintThemes).
- **Splid** — the offline/no-account specialist: group codes, no login, cached FX for 150+ currencies, one-time ~$4.99 lifetime unlock, 5.0/5 from ~86K App Store reviews — deliberately shallow (no reminders, receipts, recurring, or cross-group person ledger; every group is an island; no web app) (profile: strengths, weaknesses).

**Relevance to Hisaab: medium-high.** Hisaab's groups/splits stack (join codes and invite deep links at src/App.tsx:449; ad-hoc SplitWithSheet, commit 331a702; group settle-up PDFs per docs/statement-family-build-plan.md:57-65) competes here for the flatmate/trip job (persona Ayesha, 01-product-understanding.md §(a)). Splid's "no account, nothing to leak" is the nearest philosophical cousin to Hisaab's no-custody stance (docs/play-store-listing.md:60), and Splitwise's paywall revolt + Tricount's free-forever anchor define the pricing conversation Hisaab's "free, always, never ads" promise (src/lib/i18n.ts:475-476) enters.

### Cluster B — Western PFM/budgeting apps (YNAB, Monarch, Money Manager/Realbyte, Wallet, Spendee)

- **YNAB** ($14.99/mo / $109/yr, no free tier) owns the budgeting *method* high ground; it is the closest philosophical cousin to Hisaab's "manual entry as mindfulness" framing, but is US/UK-sync-centric, has "no interpersonal-debt, ROSCA, or split-with-non-users concepts at all — money is assumed to move through banks" (profile: weaknesses), and ships no consumer-facing in-app AI as of 2026.
- **Monarch** (Core $99.99/yr, Plus $199/yr) is the aggregation-first benchmark with the most productized consumer PFM AI assistant — the model for Hisaab's premium-AI-gate mock (docs/design-extract/ai-tab-3.jsx:258) and the source of Hisaab's flex-budget design (project memory: monarch inspiration). Its #1 complaint is aggregator sync dropouts (Trustpilot ~2.2/5) — the exact dependency Hisaab's "no bank passwords, no sync to break" pitch (src/pages/OnboardingPage.tsx:158-160) is built to avoid. Manual entry is second-class; US/CA institutions only; useless for Gulf/Pakistani banks (profile: weaknesses).
- **Money Manager (Realbyte)** — 50M+ installs — is Hisaab's closest *functional* analogue on the tracker core: numpad-first ~3-tap entry (the bar Hisaab's QuickEntry must meet), double-entry reconciliation, fully offline and private by default, a passcode lock **that actually works** (profile: strengths — contrast Hisaab's settable-but-never-enforced PIN, SettingsPage.tsx:275-298, PinLockScreen.tsx:12 with zero importers), one-time ~$4-5 pricing. But it is strictly single-player: no shared ledgers, splits, or debt objects — "people are faked via categories/accounts" — and no ROSCA even though its home market has kye/gye culture (profile: weaknesses).
- **Wallet (BudgetBakers)** is the only Western PFM with a named-person **Debts** feature — the nearest mainstream analogue to udhaar — but it is single-sided ("a memo, not a two-party ledger"), with no splits, no ROSCA, no non-user reach, and a bank-sync reliability complaint stream (profile: strengths, weaknesses). Its multi-currency support brushes Hisaab's AED/PKR corridor.
- **Spendee** — shared wallets with payer attribution but **no balances-owed computation** ("shared wallet ≠ split ledger" — profile: collaboration), visibly stagnant 2024-2026.

**Relevance: medium.** Hisaab's full_tracker mode (accounts, transactions, budgets, subscriptions, goals, investments — src/App.tsx:458-471) overlaps this cluster feature-for-feature, but none of the five can model udhaar with schedules, kameti, or a counterparty who never installs anything.

### Cluster C — South-Asian ledger & committee apps (Khatabook, OkCredit, CreditBook, Udhaar Book, DigiKhata; Oraan + kameti trackers; JazzCash/easypaisa as expectation-setters)

- The khata apps digitized the merchant bahi-khata at massive scale (Khatabook: ~$187M raised, tens of millions of downloads, Rs 100 crore+ FY24 revenue; OkCredit: 5M+ active merchants; DigiKhata: 5M+ claimed users) on one shared design: **one-sided ledger** — the merchant records what the customer owes with no consent or account needed from the customer — powered by the free-SMS-reminder growth loop (profiles: Khatabook, OkCredit). Every one of them is merchant-framed: no consumer-to-consumer udhaar, no kameti, no expense/budget layer, no multi-currency/diaspora story (all five profiles list this identically under weaknesses).
- Their economics are the cautionary tale: the free ledger never monetized directly. Khatabook is still loss-making (Rs 116 crore net loss FY24) and pivoted to lending distribution; OkCredit's P2P-adjacent product was shut by RBI rules in Jan 2025; **CreditBook — the Pakistani proof point — could not monetize the standalone khata and became a licensed SME financing platform** using ledger data as underwriting signal; Udhaar Book monetized only by adding SBP-regulated payments and agent-style commissions (recharge at up to 2%) — "a path Hisaab's strict no-custody stance deliberately forecloses" (profiles: CreditBook, Udhaar Book weaknesses).
- **Kameti digitizers** bracket Hisaab's kameti feature: Oraan (600K+ users, mostly women, SECP-registered) solved the stranger-trust problem by *becoming custodian* — pooling money, vetting members, guaranteeing payouts, carrying regulatory weight and fees; the tracker-only tail (Golak, Kameti.pk, Kameti Digital Guru) confirms the record-keeping niche exists but is unfunded and low-polish with "no fairness proofs, no witness/verification mechanism, no cross-user consent model" — "none combines kameti with udhaar, splits, and personal finance the way Hisaab does" (profile: Oraan cluster). The profile's caveat cuts the other way too: DAWN's committee-fraud reporting shows the user's deepest anxiety is trust, "a custodial guarantee answers it more directly than a witness link."
- **JazzCash/easypaisa** (~70M accounts, easypaisa ~20M MAU as Pakistan's first licensed digital retail bank) are not tracker competitors but set the grammar Hisaab's users arrive with: phone-number-is-the-account, OTP, Urdu UI, SMS confirmation of every rupee, and an expectation of a "pay" button inside anything financial (profile: strengths, weaknesses). They move money but "do not model informal obligations" — no udhaar ledger, no qist on interpersonal loans, no kameti fairness tooling, no split logic.

### Verdict: which cluster does Hisaab actually compete in?

**Hisaab competes in Cluster C's whitespace — the consumer/social side of South-Asian informal finance — using Cluster A/B mechanics.** The khata incumbents are merchant tools whose monetization (lending/payments) pulls them further from consumers; the Western apps will not build roman-Urdu kameti/udhaar constructs; Oraan is regulatory-locked into custody. No researched competitor offers: two-sided consent-based interpersonal loans (accept/reject in /inbox — src/pages/InboxPage.tsx:39; supabase-migration-cross-user-account-effects.sql), tracker-only kameti with a provably-fair draw and a no-account public witness link (src/App.tsx:390-396; src/components/CommitteeVerifyDraw.tsx), qist/EMI on interpersonal loans, and AED+PKR dual-currency living (src/db/types.ts:1) in one product.

The real competitive risks are therefore not feature wars but: (a) **Udhaar Book's ownership of the category keyword** "udhaar" in Pakistani app-store search (1.4M+ businesses, claimed #1 — profile); (b) **a wallet shipping a good-enough social ledger** to its ~70M-account installed base (JazzCash/easypaisa profile); (c) **Hisaab defeating itself on trust** — a challenger whose entire pitch is honesty ("nothing to leak") launching with a store listing that claims a PIN lock that never locks (docs/play-store-listing.md:50 vs PinLockScreen zero importers), offline-first writes that throw behind a disabled flag (play-store-listing.md:43 vs src/lib/outboxRunner.ts:26-29 "the runner is a SCAFFOLD (all dispatch handlers throw)"), and USD/EUR/GBP it does not support (play-store-listing.md:35 vs src/db/types.ts:1).

## 3. Gap analysis — what competitors do better

**G1 (high) — Onboarding friction is worst-in-set for its market.** Every Cluster C player onboards phone+OTP in under a minute (Khatabook profile: "merchant can start recording udhaar in under a minute"); Splid and Money Manager need no account at all; JazzCash/easypaisa made SIM-number-first the national default (profiles). Hisaab demands email+password, then a hard `email_confirmed_at` gate with UnverifiedEmailScreen (src/App.tsx:87-158, 422-428), then 6 onboarding screens containing a redundant intent+mode double-decision (01-product §confusing-3). For persona Bilal (Gulf blue-collar, possibly no active email habit) this is the largest conversion tax vs every alternative he has ever seen.

**G2 (high) — Offline writes: five competitors ship what Hisaab only claims.** Splid (fully offline, cached FX), Money Manager (local-first), Settle Up (add offline, auto-sync), Khatabook, OkCredit and Udhaar Book (offline-tolerant entry + backup) all deliver real offline entry (profiles). Hisaab's outbox runner is self-described in code as "a SCAFFOLD (all dispatch handlers throw)" gated behind VITE_ENABLE_OUTBOX (src/lib/outboxRunner.ts:26-29), while the listing sells "Offline-first — log on the bus or in a no-signal basement; syncs when you're back online" in both languages (docs/play-store-listing.md:43, 96). On Gulf-construction-site and Pakistani connectivity this is table stakes, not polish; the primary persona's core scenario fails (ux-audit B2).

**G3 (high) — The counterparty reminder loop, the khata category's proven engine, is absent.** Khatabook's growth and retention were built on automatic free SMS to the customer with a balance link on every entry — "the primary collection feature and the original viral growth engine" (profile); OkCredit, CreditBook, Udhaar Book and DigiKhata all copy it. Hisaab's WhatsApp reminder is manual and sender-initiated (src/lib/whatsappReminder.ts via statement-family-build-plan.md:14), and push requires both parties on-app with Firebase setup still pending per project memory (project_connections_push_discovery). A counterparty who never installs Hisaab experiences nothing automatic — no nudge, no living balance link. This is simultaneously Hisaab's biggest missing retention mechanic and its biggest missing acquisition channel.

**G4 (medium-high) — Best-in-class settle-up math exists elsewhere.** Settle Up's fewest-transfers debt minimization with transitive routing ("you owe A, A owes B → pay B directly") and weighted member shares, and Splitwise's simplify-debts + cross-group per-person net balances (profiles), have no Hisaab equivalent: three overlapping split mechanisms produce person balances with "no unified 'who owes me' surface" beyond Contacts (01-product §redundant-1). For the group-trip job Hisaab computes shares but does not minimize transfers.

**G5 (medium) — Per-record edit accountability.** Splitwise logs who added/edited/deleted every expense in a full activity log (profile: recordManagement); Settle Up syncs edits in real time with member notifications. Hisaab has an activity feed (`/activity`, App.tsx:456) and client-side Undo/compensation (mutationSafety.ts) but no surfaced who-changed-what history per record. For a **two-sided** ledger — Hisaab's defining feature — edit accountability is the dispute-resolution layer; Settle Up's and Tricount's 2025 sync scandals (members seeing different/vanishing balances — profiles) show ledger-integrity doubt is the fatal failure mode.

**G6 (medium) — Guest members in groups.** Splitwise placeholder friends (claimable later), Settle Up offline members, Splid anonymous members, Tricount no-account link joining (profiles) — everyone in Cluster A lets a group contain people who never install the app. Hisaab's CreateGroupModal adds members only by resolved Hisaab public code (src/pages/CreateGroupModal.tsx:38-76); ad-hoc SplitWithSheet covers non-app people *outside* groups, but the group container silently requires everyone on Hisaab and nothing says so (July blocker B6, partially mitigated — 01-product §missing-5).

**G7 (medium) — Currency breadth vs the diaspora it names.** Splid 150+, Splitwise 100+, Settle Up auto-consolidation across countries (profiles). Hisaab supports 8 Gulf/PKR/PHP currencies (src/db/types.ts:1) — no USD/EUR/GBP — while its own listing claims "PKR, AED, USD, EUR & GBP" in both languages (docs/play-store-listing.md:35, 88), excluding the UK/US/EU Pakistani diaspora and the repo's own Sana persona at minute one (ux-audit B1).

**G8 (medium) — Search and reporting depth.** Money Manager ships solid ledger search/stats/export; Wallet ships reports and CSV/OFX import; even Splitwise has search, if paywalled (profiles). Hisaab has no global search component in src/ (grep 2026-09-02) and analytics limited to category drill-ins. A khata replacement must answer "what did Imran's ledger look like in March" instantly.

**G9 (medium) — A working device lock.** Money Manager ships a functioning passcode lock as a core private-by-default feature (profile: strengths). Hisaab lets users set a PIN with a success toast (SettingsPage.tsx:275-298) that never locks anything — PinLockScreen.tsx:12 has zero importers — while the listing claims "Optional PIN lock — your hisaab, your eyes only" (play-store-listing.md:50, 103). Worse than lacking the feature: it is a false security claim held by a product whose entire positioning is trust.

**G10 (structural) — Distribution gravity and the keyword collision.** Splitwise's network effect ("the default app groups already have"), Udhaar Book's claimed #1 position on the literal word "udhaar" (1.4M+ businesses), DigiKhata's 5M+ claimed users, and the wallets' ~70M accounts (profiles) all pre-empt the attention Hisaab must win with zero installs, zero analytics (10-product-analytics.md), and a brand keyword an incumbent owns.

**G11 (business) — Everyone else has a monetization mechanism; Hisaab has a promise string.** Even the "free" players monetize: Khatabook via lending distribution (Rs 100 crore+ FY24 revenue), Udhaar Book via SBP-regulated payments/commissions, Tricount as a bunq funnel, Splid via one-time IAP, Settle Up via a splittable Group Premium (profiles). Hisaab's revenue surface is one i18n string ("premium extras may come later" — i18n.ts:475-476) and a design mock (ai-tab-3.jsx:258); no entitlement, plan, or payment code exists in src/ (01-product §missing-6). CreditBook is the on-point precedent: **in Pakistan a free ledger won ~1M users but no revenue, and survived only by pivoting into licensed lending** — the exact path Hisaab's no-custody stance forecloses (profile: CreditBook weaknesses).

## 4. What Hisaab does better — the wedge

1. **The only two-sided, consent-based consumer udhaar ledger in the researched field.** All five khata apps are one-sided ("customer sees a read-only balance via SMS link rather than a mutual consent-based ledger" — Khatabook profile; "does not co-own the record" — OkCredit profile); Wallet's Debts are "a memo, not a two-party ledger"; Splitwise tracks net balances, not loans with direction, schedules and settlement. Hisaab alone combines directioned loans + qist/EMI (`emi_schedules`, ARCH-RECON §1) + consolidated oldest-first lump repayment (src/lib/repaymentAllocation.ts) + cross-user requests the counterparty explicitly accepts or rejects in /inbox (InboxPage.tsx:39; supabase-migration-cross-user-account-effects.sql). Consent is the culturally correct fix for the khata model's documented flaw (disputed balances, no shared truth).
2. **Tracker-only kameti with a fairness proof — genuinely unoccupied ground.** The Oraan-cluster profile is explicit: the tracker-only kameti niche "exists but has no funded winner, leaving room for Hisaab's provably-fair draw + public witness link differentiator," and "none combines kameti with udhaar, splits, and personal finance the way Hisaab does." Hisaab's commit-reveal ballot with verification UI (CommitteeVerifyDraw.tsx) and a witness link readable with no account, gated before all auth checks (App.tsx:390-396), sits exactly between Oraan (custodial, KYC, fees, regulatory weight) and the unfunded organizer utilities. Caveat carried from the profile: DAWN fraud reporting suggests custody answers the deepest trust anxiety more directly than a witness link — Hisaab's wedge is family/known-circle kametis, not stranger circles.
3. **Roman-Urdu as the default, not a translation.** `ur` is the app default (CLAUDE.md:50); Khatabook's 13+ languages target India, no Western app has any Urdu, and only the Pakistani merchant apps match it — for merchants. Combined with bilingual WhatsApp-deliverable artifacts (statements, receipts, settle-up plans, kameti slips — docs/statement-family-build-plan.md), Hisaab produces documents a non-app family member can actually read: reach *beyond the installed base* that only the SMS-loop khata apps otherwise have.
4. **The only conversational AI in the set, shipped free.** NL entry + AI Q&A (`/hisaab-ai`, App.tsx:476) has no equivalent anywhere in the researched field: Splitwise's AI is receipt OCR (item-level version paywalled), Settle Up's is category autofill, Monarch's assistant costs $99-199/yr, YNAB ships none. (Own-goal: the differentiator is buried one tab from the FAB — ux-audit:141.)
5. **The anti-Splitwise pricing story at the exact moment of the Splitwise exodus.** The profile calls the daily-cap paywall "the single biggest driver of user churn and of the current wave of 'Splitwise alternative' apps," and Tricount is already farming refugees with an importer. 7. **Two products in one codebase.** splits_only ledger mode (no accounts, transaction rows with both account ids null — CLAUDE.md:44-46) plus full_tracker lets Hisaab be a Splid-weight social ledger *and* a Money-Manager-weight personal tracker; no researched competitor spans both (Money Manager has no social layer; Splid has no personal layer).
