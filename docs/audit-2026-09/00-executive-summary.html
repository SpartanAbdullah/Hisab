Commit 2248327 (main) Dated 2026-09-02 13 reports · 97 agents Crit/high claims adversarially verified

Do not launch publicly in the current state

A genuinely differentiated product on an unusually disciplined codebase, sitting on an unlaunchable operational and security foundation. The wedge is real — nobody else combines consent-based udhaar, provably-fair kameti, roman-Urdu-first framing, and free AI entry. The craft is real — strict TypeScript, one data-access layer, ~90 test files over the money math. But the audit confirmed 1 systemic critical, 2 money-corruption criticals, 1 false-claims cluster, and ~20 confirmed highs. Most fixes are small-to-medium effort: roughly 3–5 focused weeks, plus one afternoon action that gates everything else.

Three Things Make It Unlaunchable

The production security state is unprovable

The entire trust boundary lives in 40 hand-applied SQL migration files with no runner, ledger, or drift detection. Under the baseline schema, any user can self-insert into any group; the fix exists only in an unverified migration, and the prod verification SQL has never been run.

05-security · 04-supabase · 02-repository

Confirmed cross-user money corruption

Account deletion hard-deletes a user's expenses inside shared groups — an owner's deletion destroys the whole group ledger. Ex-members can rewrite settlement history. Two-device edits lose loan repayments. Flaky networks can leave money half-moved with no repair queue.

03-performance C1 · 05-security H4 · 12-qa

The trust marketing is falsifiable in week one

The Play listing claims a PIN lock (dead code — zero importers), offline-first entry (all offline writes throw), and USD/EUR/GBP (unsupported). For a product whose moat is honesty, discoverable false security claims are the fastest kill-shot.

1Critical

Unprovable production schema — 40 hand-applied migrations, no runner or ledger

Baseline RLS lets any authenticated user self-insert into any group as a connected member; the fix exists only in an unverified migration. 2Critical

Account deletion destroys shared-group ledgers via FK cascade

A member's deletion hard-deletes their expenses inside shared groups, silently rewriting everyone's balances; a group owner's deletion cascades away the entire group. No tombstone, no notification — while leave_group carefully blocks unbalanced exits. Confirmed by both adversarial verifiers.

3Critical

Three false store-listing claims in a trust-positioned product

PIN lock settable with a success toast but never enforced (PinLockScreen has zero importers); "offline-first" while every offline write throws behind a disabled flag; USD/EUR/GBP claimed but unsupported. The listing itself asserts "every feature named is a shipped feature."

4High

Money can be left permanently half-moved on flaky networks

Multi-leg mutations are sequential client calls; compensation and refetch die in the same outage; no persisted repair queue exists. A mid-transfer drop debits the source and never credits the destination — routine, not exceptional, on the target market's networks. Borderline critical.

5High

Ex-members can fabricate, rewrite, and hard-delete shared group ledger rows

A leftover FOR ALL authorship-only policy on group_settlements ORs past the membership checks; UPDATE/DELETE policies never check membership; the validation trigger skips amount-only updates. In splits_only mode, this ledger is the entire money record.

6High

Cross-user trust predicates are client-writable

Consent can be forged by one PATCH on linked_profile_id; owners can conscript any user into a group and wedge the exit shut; the invite token hash is member-readable and is the accepted credential; co-members can push arbitrary app-branded FCM notifications — phishing through the app's own trust chrome.

7High

Rate-limit theater, user enumeration, and identity spoofing

The join-code brute-force limiter is a structural no-op (failure rows rolled back by the same RAISE); profile-code lookup is unthrottled — full user-base enumeration; anyone can claim any phone number and the UI decorates it with a VerifiedBadge; sign-out silently fails to end sessions on network failure.

Concurrency safety exists only for account balances

Loans are absolute read-then-write (two-device repayments lose updates while payment rows persist); the group-expense version column is stored but never compared; the settlement cap is client-side TOCTOU; double-tap can duplicate a cross-user debt request; the kameti ballot is re-rollable after witnesses saw the draw.

9High

The data layer defeats itself: silent history loss, stale balances, linear cost growth

Unbounded selects are silently truncated (~1,000 rows) and the mirror deletes the overflow locally — invisible loss of older history at about a year of use. Every money write voids the sync cursor, triggering a full-table refetch per entry; the resume refresh omits exactly the money tables; 14–21 queries fire per app switch.

Cross-user udhaar — the flagship feature — is dead for 6 of 8 currencies

The linked request/settlement tables hard-CHECK currency IN ('AED','PKR') while the app ships eight. A Saudi, Qatari, or Filipino user — explicitly targeted personas — fills the whole form, hits a raw Postgres error, and no loan is recorded.

Risks & Opportunities

Top Risks

  1. Trust collapse at launch — false PIN/offline/currency claims falsifiable by any tester in week one; Play misrepresentation exposure.
  2. Failed due diligence on schema provenance — no acquirer can verify which of 40 migrations production runs.
  3. A cross-user money-corruption incident going WhatsApp-viral in the tight-knit communities whose records back real cash settlements.
  4. Fraud & harassment platform liability — phone impersonation with a verified badge, app-branded phishing pushes, and zero block/mute/report.
  5. Launching blind — no analytics, no feedback channel, Sentry blind to the money layer; regressions detectable only via one-star reviews.
  6. Scaling wall at modest MAU — full refetch per write, ~3M+ requests/day at 10k users; the app slows with faithful use.
  7. Bus factor 1 across the entire ops surface — one human, prod-first SQL, one machine, an unrecoverable keystore.
  8. Monetization void — a promise string and a mock; the CreditBook precedent shows the free-ledger dead end.
  9. Market-entry friction — heaviest onboarding in the set vs a phone+OTP-trained market; "Urdu-first" actually defaults to English.
  10. Android version skew — no OTA path plus prod-first schema changes; deep links, App Links, and FCM broken in the current checkout.

Top Opportunities

  1. Make the trust story true, then lead with it — the no-sync positioning is validated by incumbents' top complaint stream.
  2. Lead ASO with kameti + provably-fair draw + witness link — the only unoccupied category in the research (after making the ballot binding).
  3. Automatic counterparty loop — WhatsApp share at save + a per-person living balance link; the khata category's proven growth engine.
  4. Phone/OTP onboarding and collapsing the intent/mode double-decision to match the market's trained entry grammar.
  5. Guest (non-app) members in groups — every split competitor has it; removes "everyone must install Hisaab."
  6. Splitwise-refugee capture — the daily-cap churn wave is open now; free-forever positioning plus an importer.
  7. Ship analytics + feedback before scaling acquisition — the full PostHog design exists in report 10 at ~4–5 dev-days.
  8. Premium AI tier as first monetization — proven willingness-to-pay, honors the free-tracking promise, avoids the custody trap.
  9. Debt-minimization settle-up + one unified "who owes me" surface netting the three split mechanisms.
  10. "Settle outside, record inside" rail chips + per-record edit history — the dispute-resolution layer a two-sided ledger needs.

What Could Not Be Verified

  • Live Supabase state is the #1 unknown — which of the 40 migrations production actually runs gates most security conclusions. Several "fixed" claims are conditional on it.
  • Push pipeline liveness — Firebase project, google-services.json in the shipped AAB, edge-function deployment and secrets.
  • Two July blockers were verified by no phase — B5 (goals silently losing money) and B7 (AI advice corrupting data); the Hisaab AI implementation itself was never audited.
  • Real-device behavior — keyboard occlusion, OEM reminder durability (Xiaomi/Oppo/Vivo), dark-mode rendering, sub-$150-hardware cold start: all code-inferred, none device-tested.
  • Contact-discovery privacy reconciliation — every unlinked contact's phone number is auto-submitted to the discovery RPC; nobody reconciled this against the published data-safety text.
  • Native-speaker roman-Urdu review and the moderated usability protocol from report 06.
